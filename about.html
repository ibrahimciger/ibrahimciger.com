ACADEMIC RECORD
Master's Thesis · Islamic Law & Finance

An Investigation on the
Economical Life of
the Holy Prophet

Halil İbrahim Ciğer · Istanbul University · 2015
Preface

This thesis examines the economic dimensions of the Prophet Muhammad's life through a rigorous interdisciplinary lens, drawing on classical Islamic sources and contemporary economic analysis.

The study aims to illuminate neglected dimensions of prophetic biography — specifically the economic and commercial activities of the Prophet — using methodologies from both Islamic jurisprudence and modern economic science. The research reveals a coherent and principled economic framework embedded within the prophetic tradition.

Chapter I · Pre-Prophetic Period

Examines trade practices in 6th-century Arabia, the Prophet's early commercial activities as a merchant and trade agent for Khadijah, partnership arrangements (mudaraba), and the ethical principles he established before his prophethood — principles that would later form the backbone of Islamic commercial law.

Chapter II · The Medina Market

Analyses the construction of the first Islamic market in Medina, the prohibition of monopolistic and deceptive practices, introduction of standardised weights and measures (the mudd and sa'), and the foundational principles of Islamic economic justice that distinguished the Muslim community's commerce.

Chapter III · Fiscal Policy

Covers the administration of zakat as a redistributive institution, the rules governing spoils of war (ghanimah) and their equitable distribution, land taxation (kharaj), and the Prophet's sophisticated approach to public finance — a system that balanced communal welfare with individual property rights.

Chapter IV · Legacy & Modern Relevance

Discusses the enduring influence of prophetic economic principles on Islamic finance, waqf (endowment) institutions, profit-sharing structures, and contemporary Muslim-majority economies. The chapter draws a line from 7th-century Medina to modern instruments such as sukuk and participation banking.

Conclusion

The Prophet's economic model constitutes a coherent system that balances spiritual imperatives with pragmatic governance. Its emphasis on transparency, prohibition of exploitation, and communal solidarity offers a framework with continued relevance for Islamic economics and ethical finance today.

"A principled economic model rooted in justice, transparency, and communal solidarity."
