Master's Thesis Islamic Law and Finance

An Investigation on the Economical Life of the Holy Prophet

Halil Ibrahim CigermiddotIstanbul Universitymiddot2015
Preface

This thesis examines the economic dimensions of the Prophet Muhammad's life through a rigorous interdisciplinary lens, drawing on classical Islamic sources and contemporary economic analysis.

The study illuminates neglected dimensions of prophetic biography using methodologies from both Islamic jurisprudence and modern economic science.

Chapter I Pre-Prophetic Period

Examines trade practices in 6th-century Arabia, the Prophet's early commercial activities as a merchant and trade agent for Khadijah, partnership arrangements (mudaraba), and the ethical principles he established before his prophethood.

Chapter II The Medina Market

Analyses the construction of the first Islamic market in Medina, the prohibition of monopolistic and deceptive practices, introduction of standardised weights and measures, and foundational principles of Islamic economic justice.

Chapter III Fiscal Policy

Covers the administration of zakat as a redistributive institution, rules governing spoils of war and their equitable distribution, land taxation (kharaj), and the Prophet's approach to public finance.

Chapter IV Legacy and Modern Relevance

Discusses the enduring influence of prophetic economic principles on Islamic finance, waqf institutions, profit-sharing structures, and contemporary Muslim-majority economies. Draws a line from 7th-century Medina to modern instruments such as sukuk and participation banking.

Conclusion

The Prophet's economic model constitutes a coherent system balancing spiritual imperatives with pragmatic governance, with continued relevance for Islamic economics today.

"A principled economic model rooted in justice, transparency, and communal solidarity."
